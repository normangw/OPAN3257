Assignments & Semester Project

A preview of what's coming — official instructions, datasets, due dates, and grading live on Canvas.

This page is a preview, not the assignment itself. Full instructions, starter files, and due dates are posted on Canvas as each one is assigned — check there for anything graded.

The five assignments

Each carries forward the business arc of the course, individually submitted with team discussion allowed (Type B).

Assignment 1 · The Catalog & the Customer

Single-table analysis of a retail product catalog and a CRM export.

Skills: SELECT, WHERE, text functions, arithmetic.

Hint to get ahead: practice filtering and cleaning text on the menu table in the sandbox — that's the exact skill set.

Assignment 2 · The Monday-Morning Report

The recurring KPI questions every operator asks: revenue by region, top products, repeat-customer share.

Skills: GROUP BY, HAVING, aggregates, ORDER BY/LIMIT.

Hint to get ahead: the "Customers & orders" exercises on Practice use the same pattern — total spend per customer.

Assignment 3 · Moneyball

The original data-driven hiring problem: join player, salary, and performance tables to find undervalued talent.

Skills: multi-table JOINs.

Hint to get ahead: review the JOIN example on Topics — connecting a "who" table to a "what happened" table is the whole idea.

Assignment 4 · Packages, Please

Trace lost parcels through a last-mile delivery database — detective work with joins and subqueries.

Skills: JOIN + subqueries.

Hint to get ahead: try the "advanced" subquery exercise on Practice (top-spending customer without hardcoding a number).

Assignment 5 · The SQL Gauntlet

Ten progressively harder analytical queries over a large multi-table dataset, run in DuckDB. Your capstone in place of a final exam — there is no final exam in this course.

Skills: everything, at warehouse scale.

Mini-Challenges

Six short, individual, in-class problems (lowest score dropped): a campus coffee-shop menu table, extracting fields from a customer table, fraud/returns-screening filters, building and populating an inventory table, a JOIN store analysis, and a GROUP BY customer–order analysis. The sandbox on Practice is good warm-up for all six.

Semester Project: pick your industry, run its data

Working in teams of 3–4, you'll act as an analytics consulting group reporting to a COO: design and build a relational database for a real operating business, query it to locate operational bottlenecks, and ship the results as a live Streamlit dashboard. One project, two parts — a midterm design/build submission, then a final analyze/recommend/deploy submission with an individual oral defense.

Track R · Retail & E-Commerce

~100k real orders across eight linked tables (Olist Brazilian e-commerce). Where do delivery promises slip? Do late deliveries cost review stars?

Track D · Last-Mile Delivery & Logistics

Depots, drivers, vehicles, routes, packages, delivery attempts. What's the failed-first-attempt rate, and what does redelivery cost?

Track H · Hospitality & Marketplace

An Inside Airbnb city extract. Which neighborhoods are supply-constrained? What separates high-occupancy hosts?

Teams may also propose their own dataset with instructor approval — including healthcare operations.

Note on AI tools: the course provides a 24/7 AI tutor to help you learn SQL, but generative AI tools are not permitted on the Semester Project itself (Parts 1 and 2). Credit is earned through queries you can defend orally.
